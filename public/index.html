Rates sit above the SCHADS Award for comparable classifications, and include the 12% superannuation guarantee — on weekdays the starting $53.35/hr is $47.63/hr cash plus $5.72 super. Sleepovers: you receive the SCHADS sleepover allowance, plus at least four hours of rostered work or pay immediately before or after the sleepover; any work performed during the sleepover is paid at the applicable overtime rate, with a minimum payment of one hour. The two columns above are the bottom and the top of the pay ladder — here's how you move between them.

The pay ladder

Your share goes up the longer you stay

You're paid a share of what the NDIS pays for the hour — not a rate we invented, and not what's left over after we've taken ours. That share starts at 72.5% and rises to 76.5% as your hours add up. Four rungs, the same for everyone, thresholds published right here.

Dollar figures are one weekday daytime hour including 12% superannuation. Every other rate — evenings, nights, Saturdays, Sundays, public holidays — moves by the same percentage, so the whole table above lifts with you.

Hours are counted over a rolling year

Not since you joined, and not since January — the twelve months behind you, recalculated every month. A break doesn't send you back to the start.

You move up straight away

Cross the line and the very next shift you accept is priced at the new share. Nobody has to nominate you and there's no review to wait for.

Moving down is slow, and you'll see it coming

You'd have to sit under the threshold for 90 days first. Then it's one rung, with 28 days' written notice, and never more than one rung in a 90-day period. There is no button in our system that lowers anyone's tier by hand.

The clock stops for leave

Parental leave, carer's leave, workers compensation, long-term illness — your hours are frozen where they are, not counted as zero. Breaking your wrist should not cost you your pay rate.

The Award is a floor under all of it

On a few supports the published maximum price is low enough that a percentage of it would fall under the SCHADS minimum for your classification. When that happens we pay the Award, not the share — and your payslip says which one you got.

Super, penalties and cover on top

Superannuation at 12% is included in every figure on this page. Evening, night, weekend and public holiday penalty rates are on top of your share, as is the per-kilometre allowance when you drive.

Home / Personal care / Specialist supports

Specialist supports

Eight supports need a provider registered for them specifically. If one of them is part of your day, we arrange it and work alongside the provider who delivers it — you don't get sent away and you don't end up managing two disconnected teams.

Which supports these are

The NDIS Practice Standards set out eight high intensity daily personal activities in Supplementary Module 1, registration group 0104. They are the supports where getting it wrong is a clinical event rather than a bad shift, so a provider has to be assessed against a separate standard for each one before it can deliver it.

What happens if you need one

1
You tell us

There's a question about it on the sign-up form, and the same list stays on your account page so you can change it whenever your needs change. Most people tick nothing on it.

2
We call you — before anything is booked

Usually within one business day. Not after a worker has already been rostered and is standing in your bathroom being asked to do something they can't.

3
We introduce you to a provider registered for it

You engage them directly, so your agreement is with the people actually delivering that support and your plan is charged by them for it. We don't take a margin on the introduction.

4
We keep the rest of your week

Personal care, daily tasks, household, community, transport, employment — all of it carries on with us, rostered around their visits rather than clashing with them. One roster, two providers, coordinated by us.

5
It goes on our register

Every request for a support we don't hold is recorded the moment it's made, with the date and what we did about it. That register is part of what our auditor looks at. It's also how we know whether we should be registering for a support ourselves.

Why we don't just say we do it

Because saying it would be the offence. Section 13C of the NDIS (Provider Registration and Practice Standards) Rules 2018 puts a condition on every 0104 registration:

the provider must not provide a support mentioned in Schedule 2 if the support is not set out in the provider's certificate of registration.

It's granted support by support, not as a single tick, and delivering one that isn't named on the certificate breaches a condition of registration rather than being a stretch of scope. From 1 January 2027 the platform conditions go further and make what a provider displays checkable in its own right. How we check every worker

Nothing appears on a worker's profile until someone on our team has verified it themselves. Here's exactly what that means, and what happens when a check lapses.

The two checks we do for everyone

Every person who takes a booking through BookIt holds a current NDIS Worker Screening Check, and we confirm the clearance itself with the screening unit rather than taking the card at face value. A card can still read 2029 after a clearance has been suspended, so the date on the document is not the thing we rely on.

We also check each worker against three separate banning orders registers — the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission's register, aged care banning orders made under the Aged Care Act 2024, and aged care orders made under the earlier Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission Act 2018 before that Act commenced. All three are named in the conditions, and a clear answer on one says nothing about the other two. We check again on a rolling cycle rather than once at induction — because the registers change, and a check done two years ago tells you about two years ago. What we found on each, and roughly when we looked, is written on the worker's own profile.

What "verified" means on a profile

A credential shows on a profile only when a person on our team has looked at it and recorded how they satisfied themselves — whether they sighted the original, checked the number against the issuing register, or confirmed it with the issuer directly. That record is kept, and it is what an auditor sees.

If we haven't verified something, it isn't listed. There is no third state where a claim sits on a profile with a tick beside it because a worker typed it in. That was true of an earlier version of this site and it isn't any more.

1
Documents come in

A worker uploads their screening check, ID, training certificates and qualifications through their own account. Nothing they upload is visible to participants at this point.

2
We verify each one

Someone on our team checks the document and records how — sighted original, sighted copy, checked against the issuing register, or confirmed with the issuer — plus a reference and any note. Only then does it appear on the profile.

3
We confirm the clearance and check the registers

Separately from the document, we confirm the worker screening clearance with the screening unit, and check all three banning orders registers — NDIS, Aged Care Act 2024, and the earlier ACQSC Act. Every answer is recorded on its own, with its own date and source.

4
The profile goes live

A profile can only be approved once all of the above is in place. This can't be waived — not for a busy week, not for a worker we know well.

5
We keep watching

Expiry dates are tracked automatically and workers are reminded at 30 days, 7 days and on the day. Each banning orders re-check is chased before it falls due, register by register.

6
If something lapses, the profile comes down

An expired check, a suspended or revoked clearance, a banning order, or an overdue register check all withdraw the profile automatically — off search, unbookable, unmessageable. It comes back by itself once the problem is fixed.

Why we do it this way

From 1 January 2027, providers registered for 0137 NDIS Digital Platform Service carry conditions of registration set by the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission:

worker screening check requirements ensuring that only persons who hold a worker screening clearance may use the platform; and requirements to check and display certain information about persons providing supports on the platform, such as whether a banning order against a person is in force.

We read the first as a gate on using the platform, not a filing obligation — so an uncleared worker isn't hidden from search while still able to take a booking; they can't do either. And we read the second as it's written: check and display, worker by worker. Both are built into how BookIt works rather than added on top of it.
