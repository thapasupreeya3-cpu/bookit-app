The agreement records the dwelling(s) covered and the participant(s) residing (or "all current and future residents").

Background

One or more NDIS participants reside, or will reside, in Specialist Disability Accommodation enrolled dwelling(s) operated by the SDA Provider, and receive supported independent living supports from the SIL Provider. The NDIS Practice Standards and the SDA Rules require the two providers to collaborate so that the participant experiences seamless, safe support — while their tenancy and their supports remain strictly separate choices.

Section 1. Principles

The participant comes first: both parties act on the participant's will and preferences, support their decision-making, and respect their right to choose and change either provider independently of the other.

Tenancy and supports are separate: neither party will make, imply, or allow the participant to believe that their housing depends on their choice of support provider, or vice versa.

Nothing in this Agreement limits either party's own obligations under the NDIS Act, the Practice Standards, or their registration conditions.

Section 2. Roles and responsibilities

The SDA Provider is responsible for:

- The dwelling: condition, repairs and maintenance, fire safety and dwelling compliance.
- Tenancy management: occupancy agreements, house rules made with residents, security of tenure.
- Declaring, advertising and filling vacancies (consulting existing residents).
- Dwelling-related concerns raised by any resident.

The SIL Provider (DMHC) is responsible for:

- Daily supports under each participant's roster of care and SIL Service Agreement.
- Worker screening, training and supervision of its Workers in the dwelling.
- Support-related incident management and its own Commission reporting obligations.
- Medication management and support documentation (maintained in the BookIt platform).

Jointly, the parties are responsible for:

- Safeguarding residents from violence, abuse, neglect and exploitation.
- Emergency and evacuation planning for the dwelling and each resident.
- Coordinating responses to behaviours of concern that affect the household.
- Keeping each other informed of matters affecting residents (with consent).

Section 3. Communication

Nominated contacts (named above) are each party's first point of contact; urgent safety matters are raised by phone immediately, other matters within two business days.

The parties meet (in person or by phone) at least quarterly, and after any significant incident, vacancy, or change in a resident's needs. Outcomes are minuted and filed by both parties.

Section 4. Information sharing and privacy

Information about a participant is shared only with the participant's consent (or as required by law), limited to what the other party needs to keep the participant safe and well supported, and handled under each party's privacy policy.

Matters warranting prompt sharing include: changes in support needs affecting the dwelling; incidents in the dwelling; behaviours of concern putting the resident, others, or the tenancy at risk; extended absences; and emergency events.

Section 5. Vacancies

The SDA Provider notifies the SIL Provider of upcoming vacancies. Existing residents' needs, preferences and situation are sought (in each resident's preferred communication mode) and taken into account before a vacancy is filled, consistent with the SIL Provider's Supported Decision-Making Procedure.

Section 6. Incidents, complaints and disputes

Each party operates its own incident and complaints systems and retains its own reporting obligations to the NDIS Commission; the parties notify each other of incidents relevant to the other's responsibilities.

A complaint by a participant about one party is handled by that party and never affects the participant's tenancy or supports from the other.

Disagreements between the parties are escalated first to the nominated contacts, then to each party's director or principal; if unresolved after 30 days, the parties attend mediation before any other step. Participant supports and tenancy continue undisturbed throughout.

Section 7. Term, review and ending this Agreement

This Agreement commences on the date of last signature and continues for 12 months, renewing automatically unless either party gives 30 days' written notice.

It is reviewed annually, and whenever the Practice Standards, SDA Rules, or the residents' circumstances materially change.

Ending this Agreement does not end any participant's tenancy, does not end any participant's supports, and both parties will cooperate in good faith through any transition so the participant experiences continuity.
