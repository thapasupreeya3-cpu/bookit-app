Disability and Mental Health Care Pty Ltd | Home Risk Assessment Checklist

Part 1. Initial Assessment

Justification and outline, each answered Yes or No with further information:

- Is an in-home service required to complete the task? What tasks and duties will be undertaken in the specified home?
- Is this the first visit by any Workers to the home? If the home has been visited before, provide further information regarding any risks encountered.

High risk screening

Is there knowledge of the home situation that indicates there may be a risk to Workers visiting the home? For example: is it expected that all occupants are aware of and agreeable to an in-home service; violence; threatening behaviour; inappropriate behaviour; neighbours; mental health issues; weapons; substance abuse. Record further information and proposed controls, and rate the risk as Low, Medium, High or Extreme.

Conditional approval

I have reviewed the above information and support the continued planning of this in-home service: Yes / No. Signed and dated by the worker and the supervisor.

Part 2. Risk Assessment

For each question, record the participant or representative's response, additional information and proposed controls; whether a risk is present (rated Low, Medium, High or Extreme); and the control or mitigation strategies.

- Will the hours of 8am to 5pm be suitable for the service? If times outside daylight hours are nominated, how will this be managed and does it change the risks?
- Who lives in the home? Who is likely to be in the home during the visit?
- Access to the home and property — slips, trips and falls.
- Is the property difficult to get to? An isolated or remote location, or a high rise flat?
- Is it difficult to access parking outside the home — for example a narrow street, a steep or long driveway, acreage, or the location of the room or unit in a complex?
- Is there easy access to the home — for example slippery due to rain, or on a hill?
- Smoking in the home: can a smoke free environment be maintained during the in-home service?
- Manual handling (depending on the service, for example therapy): will any manual handling tasks be conducted during the visit? Will manual handling equipment be available in the house? Any issues with use of equipment, such as knowledge, space or suitability?
- Biological: is anyone in the home unwell?
- Animals and plants: are there animals at the home, and if yes, will they be restrained or isolated for the duration of the in-home service? Do other flora or fauna need to be considered (birds, flora, allergens)?
- Biological and chemical — Personal Protective Equipment: will Workers need PPE for the nature of the tasks or duties?
- Anything else that's relevant: are there any potential hazards Workers should be aware of when visiting the home, for example access issues or renovations?
- High risk issues identified in Part 1: what measures are in place for managing the risk of violence and aggression?

Part 3. Communication with Client

The following risk management details are recorded:

- Day and date of service, and the address of the visit
- Who is being met (names), and the name of the contact person
- Who is going (names of Workers or an external agency)
- The time of arrival at the house, and when the Worker will phone the contact person
- The Worker's phone number, the contact person's phone number, and the Supervisor's phone number

Assessment endorsement

I have reviewed the above information and support the conduct of this in-home service as per this risk assessment: Yes / No, with the reasons for the decision to proceed or not proceed. Signed by the worker completing the checklist and by the Director or Supervisor.

Part 4. During the in-home service

In-home service strategies, with any additional strategies for the visit:

- Ensure all participating Workers are aware of all risks (including the high-risk issues addressed in the preliminary section) and the controls to manage any risks. Do not take this plan into the home.
- Park the car as close to the front of the house as possible but not in the driveway.
- Be alert to any unidentified hazards and mentally assess the risk.
- Be alert to indicators of a change in the behaviours of persons in the home, for example voice tone, mood, or physical presentation and unwanted behaviours.
- If you feel unsafe at any stage of the visit, politely make a reason to leave, leave the premises and, when safe to do so, phone your Supervisor.
- Establish who is in the home and whether they are the Participant or Representative.
- Do not enter the home unless invited.
- Check whether animals are restrained or isolated, healthy, and present no health risks to you.
- Always position yourself in the house so there is a clear path to exit.
- Observe the housekeeping, condition of the property and hazards to avoid incidents (for example slips or substance exposure).

Part 5. After the in-home service

Review questions, each answered Yes or No with further information:

- Did the in-home service proceed as planned?
- Were any additional hazards or risks identified as a result of the in-home service, including barriers to entry or exit, fire hazards and environmental hazards?
- Should information be recorded regarding the home or Participant for future reference?
- Was any workplace incident reported to the Supervisor and recorded as required?
- Should home service occur in this home in the future?

The review closes with any further actions or details to be noted, signed by the worker who completed and reviewed the in-home service and by the Director or Supervisor.

Part 6. Periodic Review

Each periodic review records the date; the reason for review (scheduled review, feedback, or a change in circumstances such as address or residents); the outcome and actions to be taken (a new Home Risk Assessment completed, or no changes required with the next scheduled review date); who completed the review; and their signature.

Appendix 1. Risk matrix

| Likelihood | Insignificant | Minor | Moderate | Major | Critical |
| Almost certain | Medium | Medium | High | Extreme | Extreme |
| Likely | Low | Medium | High | High | Extreme |
| Possible | Low | Medium | Medium | High | High |
| Unlikely | Low | Low | Medium | Medium | High |
| Rare | Low | Low | Low | Low | Medium |

Consequence of occurrence

- Insignificant: no medical treatment required.
- Minor: minor injury requiring first aid treatment (for example minor cuts, bruises, bumps).
- Moderate: injury requiring medical treatment.
- Major: serious injury or injuries, or hospitalisation.
- Critical: loss of life, permanent disability or multiple serious injuries.

Likelihood of occurrence

- Rare: will only occur in exceptional circumstances.
- Unlikely: not likely to occur within the foreseeable future.
- Possible: may occur within the foreseeable future.
- Likely: is likely to occur within the foreseeable future.
- Almost certain: almost certain to occur within the foreseeable future.

Acting on the risk level

- Extreme: the in-home service is not to be undertaken. Review the in-home service with the Director or Supervisor, update systems and complete a workplace incident report if needed.
- High: only proceed with providing support and services at the workplace with the Director or a senior staff member's approval. The risks associated with the workplace must be re-assessed and other options considered. Review with the Director or Supervisor, update systems and complete a workplace incident report if needed.
- Medium: the workplace should only be used after consultation with the Director. The risks should be reviewed to take into account all the hazards involved, and must be reduced prior to the provision of services or support. If in doubt, re-classify as High risk. Provide a risk update to management as required.
- Low: the workplace is acceptable. Ensure control options are followed, and provide a risk update to management as required.
