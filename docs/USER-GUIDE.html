The whole site, explained

Every feature and every process, written three times over — once for the participant, once for the worker, once for the office. Then a section that answers the only question that matters at 9am on audit day: where is it, and how do I get it out?

Updated 13 September 2026 for v88.4.2. This edition puts office work into seven clearly named sections, with an admin-tool search, focused pages and paged record lists. It retains private participant address and arrival details, the location saved for each booking, and worker service areas. It uses the latest supplied guide as its baseline and retains its corrected travel-estimate and Google Routes setup instructions. Immediate invoices, review and payment pages, bank-transfer matching, action alerts, worker pay status and all earlier document, verification, task, calendar, automatic-holiday and withdrawal features are retained.

Start with the current workflows below. The generated route and table appendices describe the supplied source; package verification identifies the complete release. Retained price examples describe source behaviour and are not a new payment-term or payroll agreement.

This manual describes implemented functionality. Provider onboarding, live keys, real invoice delivery, actual bank settlement and external payroll remain deployment connections. Local tests use synthetic accounts and provider responses; the final release report identifies the checks actually performed.

Invoices, receipts and withdrawals

Participant address & arrival details

Open Settings → Address & arrival details. This is the private place to save where support usually starts. My profile → Your details also links here. An authorised helper first selects the participant they manage; booking access is required to manage these details.

  1. Enter the unit, street address, suburb, state and postcode.
  2. Add useful Parking, access and arrival instructions, such as which entrance to use or where to park. Keep the instructions relevant to arriving for support.
  3. Choose Save address. Saving these details changes the default offered for new bookings. It does not move visits already on the calendar.

The participant, their authorised helper with booking access and the office can manage these details. The full saved address is not added to public profiles, worker search results or open cover offers. A worker receives the agreed location for a visit once they are its assigned accepted worker.

Next actions shows Add your support address when the saved address is incomplete. The booking form also offers Add your support location. It does not stop browsing, requesting a booking, completing a shift or invoicing. You can choose a different meeting place for an individual visit or confirm it later.

Withdraw, correct and reissue

Payment tracking and bank transfers

  1. Open Admin → Money → Invoices. Each invoice shows its person, payment state, recorded amount, remaining balance, due date, email status and unconfirmed bank-settlement state.
  2. Open Money → Received payments for confirmed money. With an active assigned receiving account and a unique invoice reference, the site applies a clear match automatically.
  3. For an unmatched receipt, open Match payment, check the payer and invoice, enter the amount to apply and confirm. The amount must fit both the unallocated receipt and invoice balance.
  4. Use Money → Payment exceptions and the named office action for unresolved connection or payment problems. Temporary failures retry automatically; Retry pending payment jobs runs a manual retry when needed.

Bank-transfer tracking uses a provider-issued Zai receiving account assigned to one participant billing account. The office chooses the participant and enters the provider user, wallet and virtual account identifiers under Admin → Settings → Payment connections → Receiving account assignments; Verify & save receiving account checks their ownership, active state and AUD currency before saving the provider’s bank details.

The payer uses the supplied BSB/account details and the invoice number in their own banking app. The server verifies the received transaction with the provider, then matches its remittance reference. Missing or ambiguous references, excess amounts and payments against withdrawn/disputed invoices remain visible as unallocated money. Combined payments across different participant receiving accounts need finance reconciliation; the site does not guess a split.

Ordinary BANK_DETAILS remain available as a fallback. They do not enable bank tracking by themselves: payments to an unrelated existing bank account still need actual bank evidence and the office’s Record payment workflow. Mapped provider details appear on the payment page and in PDFs created after the mapping is verified.

Provider configured means the server has connection settings. It does not certify successful live access or actual settlement. Setup instructions are in Payment setup and operation. Card checkout and optional PayTo require the deployed Stripe keys and signed webhook; Zai requires approved active accounts and its matching server credentials.

Receipts and reminders

Confirmed allocations and recorded receipts queue a payment receipt. Overdue approved unpaid invoices receive reminders at the configured workflow’s overdue milestones. Paid, withdrawn, under-review and processing invoices are excluded. Queued means waiting for email delivery; sent means the transport accepted it. Neither means read or paid.

Automatic website security checks

The application checks the public HTTPS hostname from APP_URL, using a trusted TLS connection and hostname verification. It records the last attempt, last confirmed expiry and next automatic check under Admin → Today → Website alerts. A missing manual certificate review no longer creates a task.

  • A healthy check repeats daily and clears the certificate alert automatically.
  • A temporary connection failure retries hourly. Three consecutive failures create a website alert; retries continue.
  • A trust, hostname or validity failure creates an immediate red alert. Expiry within 14 days creates a dated renewal reminder.
  • A check older than two days is marked out of date. The display never treats an indefinitely old result as current assurance.
  • If APP_URL is missing or unsuitable, the next step says to set the public HTTPS website address in hosting settings. Monitoring then starts automatically.

These are operational alerts and do not block invoices or bookings. The hosting provider manages certificate issuance and renewal. The monitor reports what the application server can check; it is not a substitute for independent uptime monitoring.

Verification automation: setup and limits

  1. Open Admin → People → Verification → Automation settings. Select available reviewers and their capacity. Enable automatic assignment only after the reviewer list and review deadline policy are checked.
  2. For a person with missing items, prepare one combined checklist. Select only applicable items and review the wording, requested date and reminder limits.

Three things changed everywhere at once, and are worth knowing before you read any screen below.

Dates are dd/mm/yyyy on every machine. <input type="date"> renders in the browser's locale, not the page's — this page has said lang="en-AU" since the first build and Chrome ignores it for that widget, so a laptop set to US English showed mm/dd/yyyy on every date field on the site — twenty-three of them in the source. The widget is gone. Every date field is plain text showing dd/mm/yyyy, with the slashes appearing as you type, and underneath .value still reads and writes ISO exactly as before — so nothing that reads a date changed, and no date can start being stored back to front. 31/02 refuses to parse rather than rolling into March.

An expiry that cannot be true is flagged. Every document type carries the longest it can genuinely run — screening and WWCC five years, first aid three, CPR one, passports ten — and anything past it says so on the board and on the document. It warns rather than refuses: a ceiling that is wrong for one type would otherwise stop a real certificate going on file at all, which is a worse failure than a wrong date somebody can see.

A replaced certificate is grey, not red. An expired document only reads EXPIRED when nothing of its type is current. When a renewal is sitting above it, the old row is greyed and labelled replaced — because a worker who renews on time should not be shown red, and red that appears for non-problems trains people to scroll past it.

Participant, worker, coordinator — and the office

And how the site knows which one you are. Strictly there are three roles on the user row and one flag.

A participant signs up from Get started, chooses their own workers, requests shifts, and sees their own file. A support worker applies from Work with us, is not visible to anybody until four separate gates are satisfied, and then accepts shifts and writes notes. An admin is not a role you sign up for at all — it is the is_admin flag on the account row, set only from outside the interface: at boot, for accounts whose email is listed in ADMIN_EMAILS in the server's environment file (and only once that account has verified its email), or by hand with node server.js grant-admin <email> and taken away with revoke-admin. Nothing in the interface can grant it, which means nobody can escalate themselves by finding a form. Since v86.8.0 an office session that nobody has used for twelve hours is over (ADMIN_IDLE_HOURS); signing back in, with the second step, is the normal door.

The third role on the user row is the coordinator — a support coordinator, plan manager or family member who acts for a participant rather than as one. A coordinator has no file of their own. A participant invites them by email from My care web; the coordinator accepts with a coordinator account under that same email, and the link that results (account_links) carries a set of scopes — bookings, workers, plan, messages, documents, invoices — that the participant chooses and can withdraw at any time. 3b · Inactive night care — sleepovers (v86.9.0)

Where it lives: a card of its own on /services and the home page, the page /services/overnight, a row of its own (per night) in the pricing page's rate table and in its claim table, the sleepover tick on the booking form, the calculator, and the active-hours question when the worker closes the shift.

Two different overnight things carry two different NDIS prices. An active overnight shift is a worker awake and working through the night, charged by the hour at the night rate like any other shift. A sleepover — inactive night care — is a worker asleep on the premises, there if something happens. The NDIS item for it (Night-Time Sleepover Support, 01_010_0107_1_1 for personal care, 01_832_0115_1_1 for daily tasks) is one flat price for the night, $311.79 in 2026-27, whatever day of the week it is — and that price includes up to two hours of active support across the night.

Since v86.9.0 the site knows all of that. A sleepover is booked as eight hours (up to ten if the evening runs late) starting between 8pm and 1am; the form sets those when the box is ticked and the server refuses anything else. Personal care and daily tasks are the two services that offer it. When the worker closes the shift they are asked how many hours they were up and supporting — 0 if they were not woken — and, if it was more than two, what the support was. Up to two hours changes nothing. Beyond two, the extra is charged by the hour at the night rate for that day (weekday night, Saturday or Sunday) as its own line: on the invoice, on the participant's statement, and in the claim file as a second row against the night item. The worker is paid their ladder share of that extra as well as their flat share of the night. Nobody is asked to be awake all night for a sleepover price: that is an active overnight shift, and the page says so.

The public page /services/overnight explains the two in plain words with the live price; the calculator prices a sleepover; the support plan asks about overnight needs. The three numbers — eight hours, ten at most, two included — are constants at the top of the night-care block in server.js, to be checked against each July's Pricing Arrangements.

The clocks, the AI, and the self-test

Added in the Build 26 line and live in v85. Nine deadline clocks the platform was already computing and then forgetting, an AI layer that proposes and never decides, and a self-test that answers the question none of the rest can: is any of this actually running right now.

The compliance clocks

Incident obligations are separate and measured from provider awareness. The office records actual external notification/form submission references. Follow the incident procedure; the older fourteen-day-from-notification rule is removed.

Other recurring jobs still monitor complaint acknowledgements, screening staleness, banning checks, uncompleted shifts, form cadence and document expiry using their configured rules. Check job freshness and errors under Today → Website alerts. A running job is not proof that the external action happened.

The AI layer — proposes, never decides

No code path in the AI layer writes to a participant record, a credential field, an incident, a booking or a visibility flag. It writes one thing: a row saying what a model thought.

Two use cases. Credential extraction reads a worker’s upload and pre-fills the expiry, number and issuer beside the document; the person verifying accepts, corrects or rejects, and the write happens through the existing route with the existing validation. Shift-note triage reads a completed note and raises a flag for possible safeguarding, health-change, medication or scope concerns the worker did not tick the box for — it adds to the worker’s own flag, never replaces it, and records a row on a clean read too.

Everything a model proposes lands on the AI suggestions board, waiting on a person. Every run logs the use case, model, region, prompt version, the source, the raw output, confidence, timing — and then who reviewed it, what they decided, and the reason for any override. If the accept-unchanged rate sits near 100%, the self-test flags that as a warning, because it usually means the review has become a formality.

Two refusals that are controls, not warnings. Note triage refuses outright for any participant without specific recorded consent to AI processing — a separate, per-person consent, not the general privacy form. Backups — two independent layers

One for "I deleted something", one for "the machine is gone".

Nightly backup set. scripts/backup.js, run by the bookit-backup.timer between 02:15 and 02:25 Sydney time (a random delay keeps it off the exact minute the instance snapshot starts), writes one dated folder under BACKUP_DIR: a consistent copy of the database (SQLite's own VACUUM INTO, integrity-checked — never a plain cp, which can miss rows still in the write-ahead log of a running site), bookit-docs and bookit-photos as tar.gz, and a manifest with row counts, file hashes and any document row whose file is not in the archive. It keeps 35 days and never fewer than three sets, and if BACKUP_S3_URI is set it copies the set off the instance to a Lightsail bucket or an S3 bucket. This is the one you use when a row is wrong or a document was deleted. It does not touch the running service. If it fails it fails loudly: systemctl --failed and journalctl -u bookit-backup. Setting it up is ops/README.md.

Lightsail automatic snapshots. The whole instance, daily, keeping the last 7. This is the one you use when the server itself is unrecoverable: restore the snapshot to a new instance, re-attach the static IP, and you are running again with the domain unchanged.

As first shipped, the review's geography rule refused any booking outside a worker's declared service areas — and with no worker having declared any, that would have stopped every cross-suburb request on deploy day. It is now a warning the person confirms, not a wall.

A dialog says how far and how long — "This visit is about 1 h 17 min by car (87 km) between Blacktown NSW — the worker's stated area — and Wyong NSW, with typical traffic — a planning estimate, checked just now" — with See the route on Google Maps ↗, and asks for a deliberate yes.
  2. The worker sees the same estimate before accepting and confirms for themselves. They are the one driving.
  3. The booking records both confirmations — Out of area — about … from Blacktown NSW; confirmed by the participant and the worker — on the card and for the office, with the route link.

The same warn-and-confirm applies to the office's own assignment, a series edit, and a worker claiming from the open-shift feed. The automatic pools — cover offers, "next free" — stay in-area: an automatic pool never confirms on anyone's behalf.

Where the distance comes from. Two layers. Without a key, The Care Web estimates from static locality centroids shipped in data/au-localities.json (17,500 Australian localities and postcodes, extracted at build time from a public dataset — no runtime dependency, no network) and labels it an estimate from the map, not a promise. With a key, it asks Google's Routes API (Compute Route Matrix — the successor to the Distance Matrix API, which Google now lists as legacy) for the drive time with typical traffic, labels it a planning estimate and says when it was checked (the age is worked out when it is shown, not when it was fetched), keeps a traffic-aware answer for six hours and a traffic-free one for a day so the same question is not paid for twice, and falls back to the estimate if Google does not answer within three seconds. Since v88.1.5 the figure a person confirms is signed into their confirmation: what is saved on the visit is exactly what they saw, and a confirmation for a different figure, a moved visit, or a bare "yes" without the figure is refused. The route link needs no key: it is Google's documented Maps URL scheme and is free.

Turning on Google drive times — step by step

About fifteen minutes. Nothing breaks while it is off; the estimate is used until the key is in place.
  1. Open Google Cloud. Go to console.cloud.google.com and sign in with the DMHC Google account (the one that owns the Drive). If asked, create a project — call it The Care Web.
  2. Turn on billing. Menu → Billing → link a billing account (a card). Google Maps Platform gives a monthly free allowance that covers thousands of route-matrix lookups; with The Care Web caching each suburb pair for six hours, expect the bill to be $0 for a long time. Set a budget alert at, say, $10 so you would hear before it ever became otherwise.
  3. Enable the Routes API. Menu → APIs & Services → Library, search Routes API, press Enable. (Not "Distance Matrix API" — that one is marked legacy.)
  4. Create the key, restricted from the start. APIs & Services → Credentials → Create credentials → API key. The dialog now asks for the restrictions before it will create anything (it says API selection required until you choose). Under API restrictions click the selector, type Routes in the filter and tick Routes API only — if it is not in the list, the API is not enabled yet: go back to step 3. Under Application restrictions choose IP addresses and add both of the server's addresses — the static IPv4 15.134.116.135 and its IPv6 2406:da1c:18a5:9700:d62a:def4:4395:e359 (the instance is dual-stack; a v4-only restriction rejects a call that happens to go out over v6). Create, then copy the key somewhere safe for a moment. A leaked key restricted this way is useless to anyone else.
  5. Put it on the server. Open the Lightsail console, the bookit instance, Connect using SSH. Then: 
    sudo nano /etc/bookit.env
    Add one line at the end (paste the key, no quotes, no spaces around =): 
    GOOGLE_MAPS_KEY=AIza…your key…
    Save (Ctrl-O, Enter) and leave (Ctrl-X).
  6. Restart the app. Settings are only read at start: 
    sudo systemctl restart bookit
sudo journalctl -u bookit -n 30
    The log should show the normal boot lines and no error.
  7. Check it. Sign in as a participant, pick a worker whose stated area is somewhere else, and start a booking. The warning should read "… with typical traffic — a planning estimate, checked just now" and no longer say "an estimate from the map". If it still says estimate, the key is not being read: check the line in /etc/bookit.env, that the Routes API is enabled on that project, and the restriction's IP.
  8. Do not put the key on GitHub, in a chat or in a screenshot. It lives in /etc/bookit.env only, like every other secret. If it is ever exposed, delete it in Google Cloud, create a new one and repeat step 6.
  9. If Google stops answering — quota exhausted, key deleted, project unpaid — nothing breaks: the site quietly falls back to the map estimate and the warning says so. A cached Google figure is kept until it expires, then the estimate takes over.
Cost, plainly (Google's global price list, September 2026, US dollars). The Care Web asks for the drive time with traffic, which is the Compute Route Matrix Pro SKU: the first 5,000 lookups a month are free, then US$10 per 1,000. One lookup is one origin to one destination, made only when a chosen worker is outside their stated area, and remembered for six hours (a day without traffic) — so even a busy month is a few hundred lookups, inside the free allowance; a month of 10,000 would cost US$50. If you would rather the cheaper tier, set GOOGLE_MAPS_TRAFFIC=off beside the key: that asks without traffic (the Essentials SKU — 10,000 free, then US$5 per 1,000) and the answer is the road-and-speed-limit time rather than the time right now. Google has no hard billing cap by default — the budget alert in step 2 is the safety net, and you can also set a daily quota on the Routes API in the Cloud console.

Since the logo: the audit fixes and the workflow release (v88.1.3 – v88.2.0)

Between 10 and 11 September 2026 the site went through an outside audit, a verification of the fixes, a process review of the participant and worker journeys, and one large release that acts on all of it. This section is the plain account of what changed for each person who uses the site. Nothing about the services, prices, funding rules or the animated home page changed; what changed is how the site protects records and how it guides people to their next step.

Safety fixes you will notice (v88.1.3 – v88.1.5)

  • Registers are no longer open to every worker. The medicine, incident, complaints and other shared registers used to be readable and writable by any account with the worker role — even one that had only just registered. Now the office grants access to a register per worker, as read, append (add rows, never change old ones) or edit, always with an expiry date, from a panel on the register page itself. A register can be scoped to one participant, and changing the scope withdraws the old grants. After this release, workers have no register access until the office grants it — grant it before their next shift.
  • Confirming an out-of-area visit only ever waives the distance. Leave, time windows and unavailable weekdays are checked first, so a confirmation can no longer step past them. The drive-time estimate a person confirms is signed into the confirmation, so what is saved on the visit is exactly what they saw — with who confirmed it and when. A Google figure now says what it is: “about 35 minutes by car (22 km) between Ryde and Parramatta, with typical traffic — a planning estimate, checked 40 min ago.”
  • Cover offers are reviewed, then accepted against the same facts. A worker’s review of a cover offer is kept for a short time and bound to the visit, the participant’s current plan version and the travel figure; acceptance re-checks all of it and refuses if the plan has a new version, the visit moved, leave was added, eligibility was withdrawn, the offer expired or the visit was voided.
  • Sleepover active hours must be real numbers. Words, lists, blanks, negatives and more hours than the shift are refused; hours are entered in 15-minute steps and are never rounded silently; a refused entry leaves the shift open and nothing is invoiced.
  • Bigger uploads and bigger registers. Publishing a policy from a Word or PDF file and saving a register that has grown past 100 KB both work; the error, if there is one, names the real limit.
  • Two people editing a register at once no longer lose work. Added rows are merged; where two people changed the same existing row, the page keeps your entry on screen and asks rather than guessing.

Next actions (v88.2.0)

Every signed-in person now has a page called Next actions. It shows three lists — Do now, Waiting on someone else and Later — and each task opens the exact field, document or visit it is about and brings you back afterwards. A new worker sees “confirm your email”, “upload your screening” and “office checking your references” as three separate things, which is what they are. Office tasks name an actual owner and a due date. Tasks are recomputed whenever something changes: a registration, an upload, a review, a plan confirmation, a credential expiring.

For participants and their helpers

  • Private payment is a real choice. “No NDIS / private” used to be treated as missing funding, so a private participant could not book even a meet-and-greet. Private is now its own lane through quotes, agreements, invoices and statements, without NDIS-number or plan tasks. It stays blocked until the office has recorded the private billing basis and the tax treatment in Next actions → Setup. “Funding not chosen yet” is still blocked, and no existing record is silently reinterpreted.
  • A helper can do what they were allowed to do. Account links carry separate permissions for the plan, documents, bookings and invoices. A helper with plan permission can now open and save the plan; a documents-only helper still cannot. Whose account is active is shown on every task, switching between people never carries draft text across, and a revoked link fails on save as well as on page load.
  • Support-plan drafts cannot be overwritten. Every save carries a revision. If two tabs, a slow connection or a retry would replace newer text with older text, the save is refused and a recovery view shows both versions for you to choose from. A failed save never says “Saved”.
  • Enter things once. Details given at signup, in billing and in intake prefill the support plan after you confirm them; the plan is guided section by section, and the worker’s brief shows what changed since they last acknowledged it.
  • Book again, and usual visits. A saved routine remembers the worker, the day and the time — never old care notes or old prices, which are always taken fresh.
  • Fewer messages. Optional quiet hours and a routine digest group the ordinary notifications; urgent ones still come at once. A private calendar subscription (confirmed by you, revocable, expiring after 90 days) shows visit times without names or addresses.
  • Changes and endings are handovers. Changing support opens an office case that preserves existing visits; closing it requires the future-visit list, outstanding notes and timesheet questions to be resolved first.

For workers

  • Recruitment in one place — interview slots you can book or cancel, references, and employment evidence, with the office’s checks shown separately from what is yours to supply.
  • The upload tray keeps the files that succeeded when one fails, notices a repeat upload, and explains what each document is for.
  • Recurring requests are reviewed together and the dates you choose are accepted in one go — each date still gets its own plan, availability and travel check.
  • Leave shows the visits it affects before you save, and asks for cover only for the ones you select. A voluntary profile pause hides you from new bookings but lets you finish accepted work; safety withdrawals and cover restrictions are unchanged.
  • One visit workspace: the brief, what changed since you last acknowledged it, your saved note draft, actual active hours, transport, completion, questions and a linked incident form.
  • Renewals show the visits they would affect, and distinguish “replacement received” from “replacement approved”.
  • Pay status is honest: prepared in a batch, exported, or an external payment reference recorded by the office. Exporting a batch never marks anyone paid; the site does not move money.

For the office

  • The pipeline is a work queue: paginated, searchable, every task with an owner and a due date; timing starts when a stage is observed ready, not when the account was created.
  • Jobs tell the truth. A scheduled job now holds its lock until asynchronous work settles and records a failure as a failure; before this, the nightly invoice job could stamp “done” while it was still running.
  • Messages have a durable outbox. Every email is written in the same transaction as the event that caused it, sent by a job every 15 seconds, retried with bounded attempts, de-duplicated, re-checked for access and opt-outs, and parked in an exception queue the office can see. An invoice that failed to send is retried as the same invoice, never re-created. Queued means saved; sent means the transport accepted it — not proof the person read it.
  • Pay batches replace the old worker-share download (now labelled a historical register). Lines have stable IDs, exceptions and adjustments are reviewed, exports are repeatable, and the external payment reference is a separate acknowledgement. Scheduled preparation stays off until the office confirms the first date not yet processed by the external payroll.
  • Invoice exceptions show unissued or held work, the original payer and amount, delivery status and receipt evidence; a mismatched receipt stays an exception rather than being marked paid.
  • Document assistance (extracting fields from an uploaded credential) is off until the AI provider, region and consent are configured and the office approves it; each worker opts in per document; suggestions show their source text and confidence and always need a human; it never verifies a clearance.
  • Time and progress reports median and P90 waiting times per task so time savings are measured, not promised.

What runs by itself now, and what still needs a person

Automatic: task recomputation; message delivery and retries; routine digests; renewal tasks; first-meeting and first-shift follow-up tasks; invoice preparation and queued delivery; payroll draft preparation once the cutover date is set; the audit snapshot; the existing clocks for cover, timesheets and credentials. A person always: confirms declarations, reviews evidence and eligibility, resolves a failed delivery, approves a pay batch and processes it externally, records non-integrated payment evidence, reconciles a mismatched receipt, accepts or rejects an extracted field, and chooses to continue after a first meeting. Invoice balances now update automatically from matched confirmed provider receipts; payroll still uses the external process and evidence review remains a human decision.

Security and operations — added in v85.2 / v85.3
NODE_ENVset to production to turn on the fail-closed preflightemptyKnow this one. With it set, the server refuses to start unless APP_URL is https, a persistent secret exists, admin MFA is not disabled, SEED_DEMO is not on, a mail provider is configured, BIND_HOST is loopback, and the data folders sit outside public/. It fails loudly and names the reason; it touches nothing before it decides.
STRICT_PRODthe same preflight, without setting NODE_ENVoffUse whichever of the two suits your service file.
BIND_HOSTthe interface Node listens on127.0.0.1Loopback by default, so port 3000 is unreachable from the internet even if a firewall rule goes missing. Caddy is the only intended caller.

Two of these are load-bearing for security. ADMIN_EMAILS (at boot, for verified accounts) and node server.js grant-admin (by hand) are the only ways anyone becomes an admin — there is no screen for it, on purpose. SECRET signs every session cookie and every email link; if you set it once and then lose it, everyone is logged out and every outstanding verification and password-reset link stops working. If you never set it, the server generates one into .secret beside the code and reuses it, which is fine for one machine and wrong the moment there are two.
